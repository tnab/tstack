# tstack decide

First read [shared principles](../../references/principles.md). Produce a recommendation the reader can evaluate, or an accurate record of a decision already made.

## Frame the decision

Identify the actual choice, desired outcome, decision deadline, audience, and who has authority to decide. Distinguish the person coordinating the work from the decision maker and contributors. Preserve existing governance; do not appoint people or invent approval requirements.

Separate hard constraints from preferences and assumptions. Establish the criteria that matter to this choice, such as cost, time, reversibility, or quality, using the user's context. Question a framing that assumes its own answer. Ask only for missing information that prevents a useful comparison; otherwise state material assumptions and proceed.

Determine whether the task is to propose a decision or record an existing one. For a record, reconstruct the contemporary rationale without inventing reasons that seem sensible now. Distinguish later evidence from what was available when the decision was made.

## Compare credible options

Read the supplied material and its supporting evidence. Use [tstack search](../tstack-search/SKILL.md) only when additional research is needed and within the user's permitted sources. A supplied-only corpus remains the boundary. Mark unavailable evidence that could change the choice.

Compare materially different, feasible alternatives against the same criteria. Include continuing the current approach, deferring, or running a smaller experiment when credible. Do not manufacture options to reach a fixed count. If constraints leave one viable choice, explain which constraints eliminate the others.

Expose benefits, costs, dependencies, failure modes, and who bears each tradeoff. Include transition costs and reversibility where they matter. A table helps when comparisons share dimensions; prose may serve a small choice better. Do not invent weights, numeric scores, probabilities, or precision. If using a quantitative model, show the inputs, units, assumptions, and calculations.

Test the strongest alternative to the preferred option. Look for disconfirming evidence and identify assumptions whose plausible change would reverse the ranking. Use ranges or conditional recommendations when uncertain inputs dominate. Distinguish a fact to verify from a value judgment the decision maker must make.

## Recommend and record

Lead with the recommendation and the decisive reason. Explain the material tradeoffs, rejected alternatives, and residual uncertainty. If evidence does not justify choosing, name the smallest useful check and how its possible results would affect the decision. Do not recommend more research without a decision it could change.

Keep a proposed recommendation visibly separate from an authorized decision. Record approval, decision maker, date, and source only when established. Do not interpret silence or a draft label as approval. Identify practical next steps without creating assignments or commitments beyond the record.

State observable revisit conditions where useful: a changed assumption, missed threshold, new evidence, or review date supported by the context. Before delivery, check that citations support consequential claims, calculations are reproducible, and the recommendation follows from the stated criteria.

Finish the prose with [tstack write](../tstack-write/SKILL.md), preserving conditional language, dissent, and decision status.
