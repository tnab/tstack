# tstack meeting

Read and apply the [shared principles](../../references/principles.md) first.

Use the requested mode: preparation or notes. Do both only when requested. Match the audience and requested format; formal minutes may need a fuller record than a working recap.

## Establish the record

Identify the meeting, date, purpose, audience, and available material. For notes, use the supplied transcript, recording, or contemporaneous notes. An agenda establishes intended discussion, not what happened. An invitation establishes invitees, not attendance. If no meeting record is available, request it or provide a clearly labeled notes template; never reconstruct an event from its agenda.

Read only relevant background. Use [tstack-search](../tstack-search/SKILL.md) when a material context gap needs investigation within the user's scope. Keep outside context distinct from statements made in the meeting. For long records, process manageable sections and retain source locators, speaker attribution, and unresolved references across them.

## Prepare

Lead with the outcome the meeting needs and the question participants must resolve. Name known constraints, prior decisions, and unsettled assumptions that affect that question. If the purpose is unclear, propose a labeled objective or ask when the distinction changes the preparation materially.

Build a short agenda around useful discussion and decision points. Use the actual meeting duration when proposing time allocations; leave time to confirm decisions and next steps. Label suggested agenda items and roles as proposals. Do not imply that a decision maker or attendee has agreed to participate.

Provide the minimum background and linked prereading needed to contribute. Explain what input is needed from whom when known. For a decision meeting, surface alternatives, dependencies, and disputed evidence; do not quietly select the outcome. Include relevant outstanding actions when they affect the agenda.

## Produce notes

Lead with the meeting's supported outcome. Organize the rest by topic or the user's template, keeping decisions, discussion, actions, and open questions distinguishable. Preserve consequential rationale, objections, approval conditions, and dependencies. Omit conversational repetition.

Attribute opinions and commitments to their speakers. Resolve speaker labels only from evidence; mark uncertain attribution. A suggestion, request, or unchallenged statement does not establish consensus, approval, or an accepted commitment. Record conditional agreement with its conditions. When the record is incomplete, say "no decision recorded," not "no decision made."

For each action, capture the specific outcome, recorded owner, due date, conditions, and source. Mark absent ownership or dates as unspecified. Distinguish an assignment from acceptance. Preserve ambiguous relative dates rather than guessing. Use transcript timestamps, note sections, or direct links beside consequential decisions and commitments. Label later corrections or status updates separately from the meeting record.

## Verify and deliver

Check decisions, names, dates, quotations, and actions against their source passages. Flag material transcript gaps or conflicting notes. Use [tstack-followups](../tstack-followups/SKILL.md) if the request also requires reconciling actions across records.

Finish through [tstack-write](../tstack-write/SKILL.md). Return the requested artifact. Preparing it does not authorize sending a recap, scheduling meetings, assigning work, or creating tasks.
