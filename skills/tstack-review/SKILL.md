# tstack review

First read [shared principles](../../references/principles.md). Help the author fix issues that could change a reader's understanding or decision.

## Establish the review

Read the actual artifact and enough surrounding context to understand its purpose, audience, stage, constraints, and requested review scope. Identify the revision being reviewed. Judge an early proposal against its intended use, not an imagined finished specification.

When asked only to review, leave the artifact unchanged. If a review and revision are both requested, make supported changes within that scope, preserve unresolved questions, and check the final artifact again. Reviewing does not authorize publishing comments or contacting contributors.

Use supplied evidence first. Invoke [tstack search](../tstack-search/SKILL.md) only when a consequential claim requires verification and additional research is within scope. Respect a supplied-only corpus. If the underlying source is inaccessible, report the claim as unverified rather than false. A citation's presence does not prove that its source supports the assertion.

## Check what changes the outcome

Select the relevant lenses; do not run every lens as a ceremony:

- **Facts and evidence:** Check central claims against original sources. Preserve applicable dates, populations, conditions, and approval status. Look for contradictions, unsupported generalizations, and summaries that overstate the record.
- **Reasoning:** Trace evidence to conclusion. Test hidden assumptions, causal claims, meaningful alternatives, and counterevidence. Distinguish disagreement over priorities from a factual or logical error.
- **Numbers:** Recalculate consequential totals, percentages, comparisons, and units from available inputs. Check denominators, time periods, baselines, and whether a projection is presented as an observed result. If inputs are missing, identify the gap instead of fabricating a correction.
- **Practical completeness:** Check whether the proposed action follows from the problem and is feasible under stated constraints. Flag missing dependencies, decisions, owners, or success criteria only when their absence could derail the intended use. Do not invent commitments to fill gaps.
- **Reader understanding:** Flag ambiguity or structure that changes meaning, hides the main decision, or makes action unclear. Treat cosmetic preferences separately from substantive defects.

For complex work, independent reviewers can examine bounded questions if supported and useful. Read the underlying evidence behind their findings. Repeated agreement is not a substitute for verification.

## Deliver actionable findings

Lead with the overall assessment and the issues that matter most. For each substantive finding, provide an exact locator, the problem, its consequence, supporting evidence, and a specific correction or check. Use page, section, paragraph, table row, line, or a short distinguishing excerpt from the artifact actually read. Cite external evidence beside the relevant claim.

Match urgency to consequence and confidence. Separate demonstrated defects from unresolved questions and optional suggestions. Deduplicate findings and discard criticism that depends on an implausible scenario or merely reflects your preferred approach. Do not manufacture findings to fill a quota. If no consequential defects were found, say so and name any material verification limits.

Before delivery, recheck the strongest findings against the artifact and its context. Finish the prose with [tstack write](../tstack-write/SKILL.md), retaining evidence, uncertainty, and useful locators.
