# Brief

Read and apply the [shared principles](../../references/principles.md) first.

Turn the available record into an answer the reader can use. Organize around the question and its implications, rather than reproducing the documents in order.

## Frame the brief

Identify the audience, the question they need answered, requested length, and any decision the brief should inform. Infer these from the request when clear. Ask only when a missing detail would materially change the result.

Establish the corpus and cutoff date. For supplied documents, stay within that corpus unless the user requests broader research. Use [search](../search/SKILL.md) only to fill a material evidence gap when searching is within scope; give it the specific question and limits. Otherwise name the gap. Do not expand the assignment merely because a document contains links.

## Read and reconcile

Inventory the in-scope material before claiming coverage. Note duplicates, versions, unreadable files, inaccessible links, and excerpts that omit relevant context. For a large packet, map its sections and read the portions needed for the question; distinguish selective reading from a complete review.

Keep a compact evidence map organized by claim: supporting source and locator, applicable date, authority or approval status, counterevidence, and remaining uncertainty. Read surrounding context for consequential claims. Trace repeated summaries to their original source where available; repetition is not independent support.

Weight evidence by its fit to the claim. An approved decision establishes the agreed plan; direct observations establish measured outcomes; a draft or participant account establishes what was proposed or reported. A later edit alone does not replace an earlier decision. Compare definitions, populations, units, and periods before combining numbers or calling results contradictory.

Separate substantive disagreement from differences in scope or date. Preserve unresolved conflicts with both sources. Do not average incompatible claims, invent consensus, or treat missing evidence as a negative finding. Attribute minority views when they affect the answer. Label synthesis and interpretation as such when they go beyond any single source.

## Deliver a usable synthesis

Lead with the strongest supported answer, including a consequential qualification immediately. Then include only what helps the audience understand or act:

- The main findings, grouped by question or theme, with citations beside the relevant claims.
- Implications for the stated purpose, separating your interpretation from recorded conclusions.
- Material disagreements, unknowns, and the evidence that would resolve them.
- A short coverage note when exclusions or unavailable material limit the answer.

Use this structure selectively; a small packet may need only a few paragraphs. Provide a document-by-document inventory only when requested or necessary to establish coverage. Recommendations are optional and must not become attributed commitments.

Recheck the central conclusion against supporting and conflicting evidence. Confirm that dates, quantities, attribution, and citation targets survived compression. Apply [write](../write/SKILL.md) to the final brief, preserving those checks and qualifications.
