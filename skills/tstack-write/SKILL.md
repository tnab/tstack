# tstack write

Write something the intended reader can understand on the first pass. Preserve the author's meaning and the evidence behind it.

## Write for the reader

Use the audience, purpose, and requested format from the task. Lead with the answer, decision, recommendation, or action. Include background when it changes how the reader should interpret that point.

Use concrete subjects and verbs. Name who does what. Prefer familiar words and consistent terms. Keep exact technical names where precision matters. Mix sentence lengths naturally; brevity does not require fragments or clipped prose.

Use paragraphs for explanation, bullets for parallel items, and tables for comparisons. Keep headings and emphasis useful. A short answer rarely needs several sections or a concluding summary.

## Remove the tells

Cut throat-clearing, flattery, generic conclusions, repetitive summaries, vague praise, decorative formatting, and unnecessary process narration.

Replace stock contrasts such as "not just X, but Y" with the actual point. Remove forced groups of three, invented metaphors, synonym cycling, and jargon that adds no precision. Replace words such as "leverage" and "delve" when a plain verb says the same thing. Judge words in context rather than treating a blacklist as a quality test.

Replace vague attribution with an identified source, or remove the unsupported claim. Replace "significant improvement" with the supported result if known. Do not invent a number to make prose sound specific.

Prefer periods and commas when dash-heavy or parenthetical sentences become hard to follow. Do not damage a clear sentence, quotation, title, or technical expression to enforce a punctuation preference. Avoid artificial casualness and invented personal anecdotes.

## Preserve meaning

Keep numbers, names, dates, links, attribution, scope, negation, and necessary uncertainty. "May launch" must not become "will launch." A request is not a commitment; a suggestion is not a decision.

For editing, use the supplied facts. Do not add research, opinions, or stronger claims unless requested. If a material ambiguity prevents a faithful edit, ask or flag it briefly. Keep direct quotations exact; use a labeled paraphrase when rewriting them.

For new writing, ground factual assertions in supplied material or available research. Label assumptions that matter. For meeting notes, retain decisions and actions with recorded owners and dates, without inventing agreement. For slide copy, give each slide a point and retain supporting evidence or notes where needed.

## Final pass

Compare the revision with the source. Check whether you changed a claim, removed an important caveat, or lost a citation. Read dense sentences again and simplify only where comprehension improves.

Return the finished text. Explain edits only when requested or when a substantive unresolved issue needs the user's attention. This skill edits content; use the available document or presentation skill when the task also requires a formatted file.
